# Literary Gift Shops — Wholesale Outreach List

*Compiled 2026-08-06. 162 verified-open US literary gift shops for an Etsy shop selling author-themed t-shirts and gifts. Built from a 14-agent research sweep (~850 web lookups): five regional searches, a children's-literature pass, a literary-institutions pass, a completeness check, and a per-site verification pass that confirmed each shop is currently operating and found the best contact path. Herman Melville's Arrowhead (Berkshire County Historical Society) is excluded — already a customer. The Sidney Lanier Cottage (Macon, GA) was found permanently closed and dropped.*

**How the tiers work:** Tier A = professionally run store and/or heavy visitor traffic — pitch these like real wholesale accounts (line sheet, samples). Tier B = solid mid-size shops. Tier C = small, often volunteer-run house museums — lower volume per order, but the easiest yeses, and ideal for consignment starters. The companion spreadsheet (`literary_gift_shops.csv`) has every site as one row for tracking outreach.

## How museum-store wholesale works (playbook)

### 1. Know who's buying

- **Small author houses & historic sites:** There is rarely a "buyer" — the executive director, site administrator, or a volunteer shop manager makes stocking decisions. Email or call directly; decisions can happen in one conversation.
- **Larger museums:** A dedicated retail/store manager buys via trade shows, reps, and marketplaces. Expect a formal review: product quality, mission fit, margin.
- **NPS sites:** Park stores are run by nonprofit **cooperating associations**, not the Park Service. **Eastern National** (retail brand: **America's National Parks**) runs stores at dozens of eastern sites and works with 2,000+ vendors — one approved submission can reach many sites. Submit via its online Vendor Review Form (local artisans have a separate track); a panel including NPS staff judges quality, historical accuracy, and site-theme fit. Western parks: **Western National Parks Association**.
- **Museum Store Association (MSA):** The industry's trade group. Its annual **MSA FORWARD** conference & expo (Philadelphia 2026, Albuquerque 2027) is where buyers meet vendors; MSA also runs market events at the Atlanta, Las Vegas, and NY wholesale gift markets. Vendor membership gets you the buyer directory and ShopTalk community.

### 2. Wholesale mechanics

- **Pricing:** Keystone is the baseline — wholesale ≈ 50% of retail (museum stores often mark up 2.2–2.5× on gifts, so leave headroom). If keystone doesn't work off your Etsy prices, your retail is too low.
- **Line sheet:** One clean PDF — photos, SKUs, sizes/colors, wholesale + MSRP, MOQs, case packs, lead time, terms. This is the ask-for-the-order document.
- **MOQs:** Keep them small-museum friendly: ~$150–$300 opening order, 6-piece minimums per style (or one S–XL size run). High minimums kill deals with shops doing $20K/year.
- **Terms:** Prepay or 50% deposit on first orders; **net-30** for reorders is standard. Museums are slow but reliable payers.
- **Faire:** Many independent and museum buyers now source on Faire — it gives *them* net-60 terms and free first-order returns while you get paid up front, and you set your own prices/minimums. A Faire storefront makes reordering frictionless; put the link in every pitch.

### 3. Consignment: the foot in the door

Tiny volunteer-run house museums often can't risk inventory dollars. Offer **consignment at a 60/40 split** (you keep 60%) with a small starter batch of 12–24 pieces, quarterly settlement, and you handle swaps of dead stock. Once sell-through is proven, convert the account to wholesale.

### 4. Timing

- **Seasonal historic houses:** Buy **January–April** for their Memorial Day–October season; pitch in winter when they're planning, not July when they're slammed.
- **Larger museums:** Buy at winter gift markets (January) and MSA FORWARD (spring), plus a **June–August** round for Q4 holiday. Never pitch in November–December.

### 5. The public-domain advantage

Dead authors are license-free: Melville, Dickinson, Twain quotes, portraits, and first-edition art need no rights deal — a real cost edge over licensed product. **One caveat:** the *site's* name, logo, and building imagery may be trademarked. Never print "Arrowhead" or a museum's logo without written permission — instead, offer custom co-branded pieces as an upsell.
